DEP trap hysteresis: toy model

Nanoparticles in a quadrupole electrode trap, driven by a staircase of frequencies swept up and down. Move a slider and the model recomputes both sweeps (it is solved exactly, so this takes well under a millisecond). The difference between the two curves is the hysteresis.

The model

The full problem is a 3D Smoluchowski (Fokker–Planck) equation for the particle density in the DEP potential of a quadrupole of four plates (10 µm wide, 0.6 µm gap), solved with a Chang–Cooper scheme for each frequency of an experimental staircase. One sweep takes 1–2 hours on a GPU. The toy keeps only the physics that controls the measured signal, and runs a sweep in microseconds.

1. Diffusion to the trap

The trap is short range: seen from the bulk it is a perfect sink of radius $a$ on a reflecting wall, so particles arrive at the diffusion-limited rate

$$J_D = 4\,D\,c_\infty\,a, \qquad D = \frac{k_BT}{6\pi\eta b}.$$

2. Trap depth

The depth $U(f)$ in units of $k_BT$ ($U>0$ attracts, pDEP; $U<0$ repels, nDEP) is

$$U(f) = U_0\;\mathrm{Re}\,K(f)\;S(f), \qquad U_0 = \frac{\pi\varepsilon_m b^3 (V_0/g)^2}{k_BT},$$
  • Clausius–Mossotti factor $K = (\tilde\varepsilon_p-\tilde\varepsilon_m)/(\tilde\varepsilon_p+2\tilde\varepsilon_m)$, with $\tilde\varepsilon = \varepsilon - i\sigma/\omega$. For Au@SiO2 the particle is a gold core (radius $a_c$, $\sigma = 4.1\cdot10^7$ S/m) in an insulating silica shell ($\varepsilon = 3.9$), outer radius $b=a_c+t$, with the surface conductance $K_s$ of the CTAC-covered silica ($\sigma_p\to\sigma_p + 2K_s/b$). For polystyrene (PS) it is a homogeneous sphere.
  • Electrode screening $S(f) = \Omega^2/(1+\Omega^2)$: double layer and bulk in series (an RC circuit), with $\Omega = 2\pi f\, C_{DL}\,\ell/\sigma_m$, $C_{DL} = \Lambda\varepsilon_m/\lambda_D$ and $\ell = 1\,\mu$m. At low frequency the double layer on the electrodes absorbs the voltage and the trap disappears. $\Lambda$ is the Stern factor.
  • $U_0$ uses the gap $g$: it gives 245 kT for 55 nm, 1 V, 0.6 µm, against 248 kT for the maximum depth of the full 3D field.

3. Capacity

The trap volume $V_t$ holds at most $N_{cap} = \phi_{max} V_t / V_{NP}$ particles (packing). Without a trap it holds $N_{res} = c_\infty V_t$.

4. One equation for the trapped particles

$$\frac{dN}{dt} = g\,J_D\left[\Big(1-\frac{N}{N_{cap}}\Big) - \frac{N}{N_{res}}\,e^{-U(f)}\right].$$

Its fixed point is the Fermi–Dirac (packing-limited Boltzmann) content $N_{eq} = N_{cap}/\big[1 + (N_{cap}/N_{res})\,e^{-U}\big]$, so the same equation fills the trap under pDEP (capped at $N_{cap}$) and empties it under nDEP.

5. Time to leave the trap

Without $g$, the escape rate $k_{eq} = (J_D/N_{res})\,e^{-U}$ grows as $e^{|U|}$ under nDEP, without bound. But a particle in the trap still has to cross the trap size $l_t$, which takes at least $\tau_l = l_t^2/D$. Transport between bulk and trap and crossing of the trap are two steps in series (as diffusion and reaction in Collins–Kimball), so both rates are multiplied by

$$g = \frac{1}{1 + k_{eq}\,\tau_l}, \qquad \tau_l = \frac{l_t^2}{D\,(1+\max(-U,0))},$$

which keeps $N_{eq}$ unchanged. Under nDEP the force also pushes particles out (drift $D|U|/l_t$), hence the factor $1+|U|$. Deep pDEP: $g=1$, filling at the diffusive rate. nDEP or shallow trap: escape at rate $\sim 1/\tau_l$.

6. The staircase and the hysteresis

Each sweep starts from $N=N_{res}$ (no trap) and visits $n_f$ log-spaced frequencies for $t_{step}$ each, upward or downward. The equation is linear in $N$ on each step ($dN/dt = A - BN$), so it is integrated exactly: $N(t) = N_\infty + (N_0 - N_\infty)e^{-Bt}$. The plotted curve is $N$ at the end of each step.

The hysteresis comes from the fact that the trap fills at a finite rate (diffusion) and is capped (packing), while it empties fast once it turns repulsive. The up sweep reaches each frequency with whatever the previous steps collected; the down sweep reaches the same frequency with a different history. The metrics above are:

metricdefinition
Hysteresis area$\displaystyle \frac{1}{N_{max}}\int |N_{down}-N_{up}|\, d\log_{10} f$ (in decades; 1 = the curves differ by the full signal over one decade)
Signed areasame with $N_{down}-N_{up}$: positive when the down sweep holds more particles
Max gap$\max_f |N_{down}-N_{up}| / N_{max}$, and the frequency where it occurs

$N_{max}$ is the largest end-of-step content of either sweep. The output is the number of trapped particles: there is no intensity, normalisation or out-of-focus background in this toy.

From 3D Fokker–Planck to the toy

The reference simulations solve, for the particle density $P$,

$$\partial_t P = \nabla\cdot\big[D\,(\nabla P - P\,\mathbf F\,(1-P/P_{max}))\big], \qquad \mathbf F = -\nabla U,$$

with a mean-field packing limit $P_{max}$, a reflecting substrate and a reservoir (Robin or Dirichlet) far away. Space is discretised with the Chang–Cooper finite-volume scheme: the face fluxes are weighted with the Bernoulli function $B(w) = w/(e^w-1)$ of the potential jump $w$ between neighbouring cells,

$$J_{i+1/2} = \frac{D}{\Delta x}\Big[B(w)\,P_i - B(-w)\,P_{i+1}\Big],$$

which keeps the density positive and reproduces the Boltzmann equilibrium exactly at steady state, however deep the trap. Time is stepped with backward Euler (Newton iterations in logit variables when the packing term is on). The DEP potential comes from a boundary-integral solution of the screened electrode problem for each $\Omega$.

The 3D runs showed that the measured content is controlled by a few things only: the equilibrium content (Fermi–Dirac with packing), the diffusive supply from the bulk ($4Dc_\infty a_{eff}$ with $a_{eff}\approx 10\,\mu$m), and how fast a shallow or repulsive trap releases particles. The toy is built from exactly those ingredients.
