The model

The full problem is a 3D Smoluchowski (Fokker–Planck) equation for the bead density in the DEP potential of the quadrupole (four 10 µm plates, 0.6 µm gap), solved with a Chang–Cooper finite-volume scheme. One staircase takes 1–2 h on a GPU. These runs show that the measured intensity of the region of interest (ROI, the gap edges seen in widefield) is set by only a few things, and the toy keeps exactly those. One sweep runs in about a millisecond.

Fixed: $\varepsilon_p = 2.55$ (PS), $\varepsilon_m$ = water, the geometry of the 3D simulations, 30 frequencies from 100 kHz to 10 MHz with 2 s each, and $c_\infty = 10^{11}$/mL.

1. The trap at each frequency

The DEP potential is computed once on the 3D grid, per unit $\mathrm{Re}\,K$, for a table of the screening parameter $\Omega$. For any particle and voltage it is rescaled:

$$U(\mathbf r; f) = -\mathrm{Re}\,K(f)\,\Big(\frac{V_0}{1\,\mathrm V}\Big)^2\Big(\frac{b}{55\,\mathrm{nm}}\Big)^3\,u(\mathbf r;\Omega(f)), \qquad \Omega = \frac{2\pi f\,\Lambda\,\varepsilon_m\,\ell}{\sigma_m\,\lambda_D},\ \ \ell = 1\,\mu\mathrm m.$$

$K = (\tilde\varepsilon_p - \tilde\varepsilon_m)/(\tilde\varepsilon_p + 2\tilde\varepsilon_m)$ is the Clausius–Mossotti factor of a homogeneous sphere, with $\tilde\varepsilon = \varepsilon - i\sigma/\omega$. From $U$ come two numbers per frequency:

  • the equilibrium content of the ROI, $I_{eq}(f)$ in units of the bulk value, from the packing-limited (Fermi–Dirac) density $P = P_{max}/\big[1 + (P_{max}/P_{res} - 1)\,e^{U/k_BT}\big]$ weighted over the ROI. Its maximum is the capacity of the trap;
  • the trap depth $U_{max}(f) = \max_{\mathbf r}|U|$.

2. Two compartments

$I$ is the ROI, the quantity that is measured. $J$ is the near field around the electrodes, whose bulk content is $J_0$. Both are in units of the bulk ROI content. The bulk refills $J$ at rate $k_b = R/J_0$, with the diffusive supply $R = 4Da_{eff}/V_{ROI}$ (capture by a sink of size $a_{eff}$). $J$ feeds the trap at the local exchange rate $k_D = D/l^2$:

$$\frac{dI}{dt} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{k_D J}{I_{eq}}\,(I_{eq} - I), & I < I_{eq}\ \ \text{(filling, stops at the capacity)}\\[2mm] -k_{out}\,(I - I_{eq}), & I > I_{eq}\ \ \text{(release)} \end{cases}$$ $$k_{out} = \begin{cases} k_D\,e^{-\beta U_{max}}, & \mathrm{Re}\,K > 0\ \ \text{(shallow pDEP trap leaks)}\\ k_D\,(1 + U_{max}), & \mathrm{Re}\,K < 0\ \ \text{(nDEP pushes out)}\end{cases}$$ $$\frac{dJ}{dt} = k_b\,(J_0 - J) - \frac{dI}{dt}.$$

Under nDEP the near field is also emptied: $J$ relaxes at rate $k_b + k_D$ toward $J_0\,e^{-\gamma U_{max}}$. This is why the down sweep, which first spends about 12 s in nDEP, fills more slowly. Each step is integrated with exact exponentials on 10 ms substeps. Both sweeps start from the bulk, $I = 1$, $J = J_0$.

$J_0 = 42.9$, $\beta = 0.602$, $\gamma = 0.238$, $a_{eff} = 10\,\mu$m and $l = 0.8\,\mu$m were calibrated on the 3D runs and are not on the sliders.

3. What is measured

In widefield, a uniform layer of fluorescent beads at any height adds the same light to every pixel, blurred but not lost. The whole chamber column above the ROI therefore adds a constant background proportional to its height, while the ROI only sees, sharply, a layer $d_{focus} \approx 0.4\,\mu$m thick. Hence $H = H_{ch}/d_{focus}$, and the signal is

$$I_{Fluo} = \frac{(I + H)/(1+H)}{\max_t\,(I+H)/(1+H)},$$

normalised in each sweep, as in the draft. $H$ and the capacity are coupled: the floor-to-plateau ratio of the normalised curve is $(I_{max} + H)/(1 + H)$.

4. Hysteresis and fit

The curves are $I_{Fluo}$ at the end of each step. The hysteresis area is $\int |I_{down} - I_{up}|\,d\log_{10} f$ in decades. The signed area is the same integral of $I_{down} - I_{up}$; it is positive when the down sweep is brighter. The max gap is the largest $|I_{down} - I_{up}|$. The rms vs draft compares both sweeps with Fig. 2 as read by eye; the red curve is taken as the up sweep. The defaults are the fit of the saturation scenario (Λ, V0, beff, Hch).

ACEO

AC electro-osmotic flow over the electrodes is strongest at low frequency, where the double layer charges. In the toy it does not change the trap. It only speeds up the delivery of beads from the bulk to the near field:

$$k_b \to k_b\,\Big[1 + A\,\Big(\frac{1\,\mathrm{MHz}}{f}\Big)^2\Big].$$

With ACEO off, $A = 0$ and the model is exactly the playground notebook, calibrated on 3D runs without flow. With ACEO on, $A$ sets the strength: the supply is $1 + 100A$ times faster at 100 kHz and $1 + A$ at 1 MHz. The weak-flow scenario of the Fig. 2 analysis fitted $A = 0.19$, together with its own Λ, V0 and H; this is the value the toggle starts from.

Where the toy comes from

The reference simulations solve, for the bead density $P$,

$$\partial_t P = \nabla\cdot\big[D\,(\nabla P - P\,\mathbf F\,(1 - P/P_{max}))\big], \qquad \mathbf F = -\nabla U/k_BT,$$

with a packing limit $P_{max}$, a reflecting substrate and a far reservoir. Space is discretised with the Chang–Cooper scheme: the face fluxes are weighted with the Bernoulli function $B(w) = w/(e^w - 1)$ of the potential jump between cells. This keeps $P \ge 0$ and gives the exact Boltzmann equilibrium however deep the trap. Time is stepped with backward Euler, and the potential comes from a boundary-integral solution of the screened electrode problem for each $\Omega$.

The browser loads that potential, $u(\mathbf r;\Omega)$ for 91 values of $\Omega$, on the 4704 ROI cells whose focus weight is above $10^{-12}$, plus the 14 cells that hold the maximum depth. With these, every quantity is the same as in the Python toy (checked to $10^{-10}$). Source: github.com/Padisi/DEPHisteresis.
